Shark Tooth Finder

Real-time AI detection for fossil shark teeth, plus the tide and weather knowledge to know when to go.

📷 Start on the beach

Tap Start Real-time Detection on Home and point the camera at the sand or gravel. Tooth-like shapes get framed, with a vibration and a sound.

Switch between Close-up (your feet to 30 cm / 12 in, a bit lighter on the battery) and Wide (small teeth up to 1.2 m / 4 ft away). Both are free.

Found one? Press the round shutter button at the bottom. The photo keeps the place, time, size, and that moment's weather and tide.

If a box was not a tooth, long-press it and choose Not a tooth. Look-alikes at that beach show as faded boxes from then on. You can pass the memory to friends. Free.

When bright sun makes the screen hard to read, tap the sun button to turn on Outdoor Mode (center of the bottom bar on Home, top right on the detection screen). Text and color contrast go up. You can also switch it in Settings.

🚫 Teach it what was not a tooth

While detecting, if a box was not a tooth, press and hold it and mark it as not a tooth.

At that beach, look-alike shapes then show as faded dashed boxes. Nothing is hidden: they only drop out of the sound, the vibration and auto-save, and a tap brings one back. Detection itself is unchanged.

The memory is kept per beach. Lookalike memory in Settings lists each beach, and you can pause it or delete it there.

You can export the memory as a file and hand it to friends. What leaves is a number pattern for each shape plus each beach's rough position and name. No photos. All of this is free.

🖼️ Analyze a photo you already took

Analyze Photo on Home looks for teeth in pictures you already took. No need to hold the camera up: pick one shot from your photo library and it runs on the device.

Finds are framed and counted. Lower the Confidence Threshold to catch more, raise it to narrow down. Save Image and Share take the result with you. All free.

It's made for going back over the day's photos at home. To run a whole batch, tap Batch at the top right for Premium batch photo analysis.

📸 Grow your collection

Every find keeps its photo, place, time, size, weather and tide. Revisit them on the map, sort, and share photos or videos.

Tag a find with one of the 27 species in the Field Guide and it links to the Timeline (how old) and Localities (where in the world). All free.

Hunter's Journey shows counts, sizes, time of day and tide patterns, overall or per species. Medals and CSV export live here.

👣 Record the path you walk

Above the start button on Home, the My path chip sets when to record: Only while detecting, or Until I stop it.

While it records, your route is drawn as a band on the collection map, so you never comb the same sand twice. Until I stop it keeps going after you close detection, until you tap Stop on Home (3 hours at most).

The same chip opens See on the map and History. In History you can read each hunt, and Select at the top right lets you pick several and delete them together.

Free shows the last 3 hunts per spot plus today on the map; Premium keeps them all. If a path could not be recorded, the row tells you why: precise location is off, accuracy was too low indoors, or you barely moved. Your location leaves the phone only when you pick a crew and agree.

🌊 When to go

Register a favorite beach (1 on the free plan, 3 with Premium) to see high and low tides and today's grade (S to D). Bright hours on a falling tide are the sweet spot.

The 7-day forecast lines up grades and each day's best low tide. The yearly calendar marks spring tides; the trip planner ranks the days of your trip.

Each hunt logs the beach, tide, time and recent rain. As they add up, the spot report shows your own patterns, like finding teeth on the ebb.

📅 Read the card of the day

Card of the day on Home is a short read that changes every day: the history, geology, chemistry and biology of shark teeth, and how to search a beach.

Reading it is free. Cards connect to the species and localities you meet in the field guide, so you can move between the two.

The morning notification is Premium, and you can set it to any time you like.

🪨 Where the geology comes from

'Bedrock beneath you' comes from Macrostrat, which compiles the world's geologic maps (including each US state survey), and, in Japan, from the Geological Survey of Japan's Seamless Geological Map V2. Sharks recorded nearby are occurrences from the Paleobiology Database (PBDB). All are published under CC BY 4.0 and credited under each card.
